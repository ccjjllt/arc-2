- **Shapes:** Rectangles formed by 0s.
- **Key Feature:** Inside or adjacent to the rectangle corners, there are specific non-zero digits.
- **Transformation:**
    - Take a digit $d$ located inside a rectangle corner (diagonal to the corner).
    - Fill the corner cell itself with $d$.
    - Fill the two cells adjacent to the corner outside the rectangle with $d$.
    - This creates a $2 \times 2$ block of $d$'s covering the corner area (partially outside the rectangle).
    - Specifically, for a top-left corner of the rectangle at $(r, c)$, if the digit is at $(r+1, c+1)$, the output sets $(r, c), (r, c+1), (r+1, c), (r+1, c+1)$ to $d$. Wait, looking at Demo 1:

        - The "corner" in the sense of the L-shape of 0s is at (3,3).

        - Output row 3: `1 1 3 3 0...` -> (3,2)=3, (3,3)=3.

        - The cells filled are the $2 \times 2$ block ending at the corner? No, the corner is (3,3). The filled block is (2,2)-(3,3).
        - The digit 3 is "associated" with this corner. It seems the rule is: Identify the "anchor" digit near a corner. It propagates to the corner and the outside adjacent cells to form a $2 \times 2$ square at the "outside" of the corner.
    - Let's look at Digit 4 at (8,5). Rectangle bottom-left corner is at (10,3).

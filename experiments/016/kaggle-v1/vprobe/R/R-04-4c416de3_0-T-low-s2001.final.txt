To solve this ARC puzzle, we need to understand the transformation rule from the input grids to the output grids.

**Analysis of the Demos:**

1.  **Identify Containers:** In each demo, there are rectangular regions bounded by `0`s. These are "containers". Inside these containers, there are one or more non-zero digits (not 0 or the background color).
2.  **Identify the Rule:** For each non-zero digit inside a container, the transformation involves "projecting" that digit towards the nearest corner of the container's border.
    *   **Nearest Corner:** The corner (Top-Left, Top-Right, Bottom-Left, Bottom-Right) of the container border that is closest to the digit.
    *   **Projection Pattern:**
        1.  **2x2 Block Outside:** A 2x2 block of the digit's value is placed outside the container, centered on the nearest corner. Specifically, if the corner is at `(r, c)`, the 2x2 block covers the four cells diagonally adjacent to the corner on the "outside" (away from the container interior). The corner cell itself remains `0` (part of the border).
            *   Example: Top-Left corner `(r, c)`. Outside is `<r` and `<c`. The 2x2 block is at `(r-1, c-1), (r-1, c), (r, c-1)`. The cell `(r, c)` is the corner border. The cell `(r-1, c-1)` is the outermost. Wait, looking at Demo 1:
                *   Corner `(3,3)` (0). Digit `3` at `(5,5)`.
                *   Output has `3`s at `(2,2), (2,3), (3,2)`. These form an L-shape outside the corner. The corner `(3,3)` is `0`.
        2.  **Diagonal Trail Inside:** The cells on the diagonal path from the digit towards the corner are filled with the digit's value.
            *   Example: Digit `3` at `(5,5)`, Corner `(3,3)`. Diagonal steps: `(4,4)`. Output `(4,4)` is `3`.
            *   Example: Digit `2` at `(16,8)` in Demo 2, Corner `(15,7)`. Diagonal step: None (adjacent). But output shows `(15,8)` and `(16,7)` as `2`. Let's re-examine.

                *   Outside L: `(14,6), (14,7), (15,6)`.
                *   Inside: The digit is at `(16,8)`. The cells `(15,8)` and `(16,7)` become `2`. These form an L-shape adjacent to the corner `(15,7)` on the inside.

    *   **Refined Rule:**
        1.  Find the nearest corner of the 0-border container for each non-background, non-0 digit.
        2.  Fill a 2x2 block of that digit's value centered on the corner (covering the corner and the 3 cells immediately outside/adjacent to it diagonally/outside).
            *   Top-Left `(r,c)`: `(r-1,c-1), (r-1,c), (r,c-1), (r,c)`.
            *   Top-Right `(r,c)`: `(r-1,c), (r-1,c+1), (r,c), (r,c+1)`.
            *   Bottom-Left `(r,c)`: `(r,c-1), (r,c+1), (r+1,c-1), (r+1,c)`.
            *   Bottom-Right `(r,c)`: `(r,c), (r,c+1), (r+1,c), (r+1,c+1)`.
        3.  Fill the diagonal cells between the digit and the corner with the digit's value.
            *   If digit is at `(dr, dc)` and corner at `(cr, cc)`, fill `(cr + k * sign, cc + k * sign)` for `k` from 1 to distance-1?
            *   Demo 1 `3` at `(5,5)`, corner `(3,3)`. Dist=2. Step `k=1`: `(4,4)`. Filled.
